0:25 – 1:05

The problem

Stay on Network · gesture at the map
Point at the India map and the KPI strip: Clean / Weather / Hardware / Unconfirmed / Waiting. Do not click yet.

This is SkyGuard AI — live quality control for Indian AWS. We are allowed only three channels: temperature, pressure, humidity. No radar. No satellite. No wind.

A min/max gate cannot do this job. A frozen sensor at 28.4 °C looks like “stable weather.” A real monsoon cell looks like a broken instrument. The official SIH example is Palam at 55 °C while the rest of NCR is normal — that is hardware, not a heatwave. The product has to tell those two stories apart in the same hour.

What you’re looking at is not four toy stations. The LSTM was trained on 151 Indian stations. The live view is a handful so the map stays readable. Palam, Safdarjung, and Meerut sit together in Delhi-NCR. Santacruz is Mumbai — and Delhi does not validate Mumbai.

Header chips: the model is loaded. Color is this hour’s QC label, not health. Teal means we trust it.

1:05 – 1:40

How it thinks

Stay on Network · point at the color legend

Three tiers. One engine. The dashboard never computes — it only polls.

One. Physical rules: impossible range, a 10 °C one-hour jump, a missing packet. Hard fail. We don’t need neighbors for that.

Two. An LSTM autoencoder on the last 24 hours, with a per-station scaler. Palam’s “normal day” is not Mumbai’s. Unusual reconstruction error means candidate — including real weather.

Three. A buddy check on the trained graph. Inverse-distance weights. At least two usable neighbors at the same hour. If they agree → genuine weather, amber, health unchanged. If they disagree → hardware, rose. If we don’t have two neighbors, we say unconfirmed. We refuse to guess.

The streamer only tells the truth. Live faults are armed here, before the model sees the hour. Raw T, P, H are never overwritten.

Impact and close

Stay on Alerts or Network

Open How QC works only if a judge looks lost. Do not wander into Advanced.

Why this matters under Disaster Management: IMD and state agencies cannot wait for a human to notice a silent AWS. A false cyclone alert wastes response. A missed sensor fault poisons the next flood model. SkyGuard sits in front of the network and answers, every hour: trust this, or don’t — and why.

We built the full loop: 151-station catalog, clean historical replay, a FastAPI shell that never overwrites raw, production QC in-process — rules, LSTM, buddy graph — and this operator console. Isolates stay unconfirmed on purpose. We would rather say “not enough evidence” than invent a spatial call.

SkyGuard is not a weather forecast. It is a self-aware, self-healing weather network: detect, overlay, keep the raw, track health, keep the trusted stations in the operational mesh.

Thank you. We’re happy to take questions — or inject a freeze or a comms drop live if you’d like to see the other faults.
